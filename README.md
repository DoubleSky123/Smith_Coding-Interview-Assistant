## Project Description

Smith investigates how computer science students understand unfamiliar code, locate bugs, and judge AI suggestions while preparing for technical interviews. Our initial target group is university students preparing for software engineering internships or entry-level roles, with different levels of programming and AI experience.

Prior research suggests that reading code, diagnosing bugs, using AI, and checking AI output are related but separate skills, and that getting help from AI does not mean a student can explain or verify the result. Existing tools such as LeetCode, HackerRank, and ChatGPT already offer practice, feedback, and AI assistance. What remains unclear is how students combine these resources, recognize unreliable suggestions, and decide whether they can solve or explain a problem on their own.

### Research Questions

- How do students approach unfamiliar-code tasks, and where do they get stuck?
- How do they identify a bug's cause and check whether a change fixes it?
- When do they consult AI, and how do they verify its suggestions?
- How do they judge whether they can explain a solution independently?
